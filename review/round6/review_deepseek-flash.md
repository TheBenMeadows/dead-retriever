**Three highest-impact findings first.** (1) The conclusion contradicts the paper's own thesis. (2) The title and abstract apply a \(K \le k\) theorem to experiments run at \(K=100>k=60\) without saying the implication weakens there. (3) A printed number breaks the paper's stated rounding rule. Details under Q2; the prose problems are under Q1.

## Q1. Readability

The paragraphs a cs.IR reader will reread, with the sentence that stops them:

- §5.1: “The quoted-AND builder matched nothing on 294 of SciFact's 300 claim-length queries, 942 of SciDocs's 1,000 titles, 530 of FiQA's 648 questions, 37 of TREC-COVID's 50 questions and 164 of NFCorpus's 323 titles (Table 2).” Five counts in one sentence, all already in Table 2. Rewrite: “The quoted-AND builder's empty rate ranged from 50.8% on NFCorpus to 98.0% on SciFact (Table 2).”
- §5.1: “Counting only the tokens the fixed builder would keep orders all five …: 8.7 content tokens per query on SciFact, 7.3 on SciDocs, 6.2 on FiQA, 5.7 on TREC-COVID and 2.5 on NFCorpus, against empty rates of 98.0, 94.2, 81.8, 74.0 and 50.8%.” Ten numbers to make one ordinal point. Rewrite: “Content-word count ranks the five collections in the same order as their empty rates; raw token count does not (Table 2).”
- §5.4: “Three things hold in every cell for both encoders. … a cap of one document leaves the lists disjoint on 8.7 to 15.1% of queries, a cap of three on 2.7 to 6.7%, a cap of ten on 0.7 to 2.3%, and a cap of twenty or more on at most 1.7%, under 1% in 11 of the 20 cells at \(n \ge 20\) over both depths and both encoders.” This is the densest sentence in the paper. Rewrite: “At every cap and depth the ceiling rate equalled the disjoint rate. At \(K=60\) a one-document list is disjoint on 8.7–15.1% of queries, a three-document list on 2.7–6.7%, a ten-document list on 0.7–2.3%; from twenty documents it is at most 1.7% and under 1% in 11 of 20 cells.”
- §5.5: “Over the whole of SciFact, hybrid-and and dense differ by at most 0.003 nDCG@10 and 0.000 Recall@100 as printed (0.0022 and 0.0000 before rounding), all of it from the six queries with a nonempty keyword list, and on SciDocs, with 942 empty lists, by at most 0.001 in each (0.0017 before rounding).” Two collections, two metrics, two parentheticals. Split: “On SciFact, hybrid-and and dense differ by at most 0.003 nDCG@10 and 0.000 Recall@100, all from the six queries with a nonempty keyword list. On SciDocs, with 942 empty lists, the gap is at most 0.001 in each.”
- §5.3: “On all 1,406 queries the AND list held a document whose text equals the query's after whitespace and case normalisation, under the query's own identifier on 1,298 (at the top of the list on 1,296) and under another identifier on the remaining 108.” Rewrite: “On every query the AND list held a document whose text equals the query after whitespace and case normalisation: under the query's own id on 1,298 (1,296 at rank 1) and under another id on the other 108.”

Machine-written tells: the scaffolding sentences that announce structure instead of arguing — “Two questions run through this section and are kept apart,” “Three things hold in every cell for both encoders,” “The paper does four things.” Delete them; the subsections already do the work. The repeated qualification is the other tell: the “collections differ in several ways” caveat appears in §3, §5.1, §5.2 and §7, and “five collections are too few” repeats it. Say it once, in Limitations, and let the results stand. “That is close to arithmetic” and “The fix has two parts, and the second is not cosmetic” are also throat-clearing.

The vocabulary cleanup is incomplete: “instrument” survives at the end of §5.5 (“different instruments”), and “alive” appears twice. Both are the abstraction you were removing. §5.2 also has a missing noun: “Table 3 gives the buggy builder's by collection” — “counts” is gone.

## Q2. Correctness after the rewrite

- The conclusion reverses the thesis: “Whether a retriever is alive and whether fusing it helps are separate questions, and only the first can be answered from the scores.” §2 says the ceiling is “a statement about overlap, not about either retriever,” and §5.3 shows a retriever that passes the ceiling and the row count while returning one useless document. Rewrite: “Overlap and effectiveness are separate questions. The fused scores answer only the first, and only once the row counts say what the overlap means.”
- The abstract says “a result list whose maximum fused score is \(1/k\) contains no document that both retrievers returned,” then reports 22,362 runs at \(K=100>k=60\). At that depth only the converse holds; that is why two ceilings with a shared document appear. Add one sentence: “At \(K>k\) only one direction survives: a maximum above \(1/k\) proves agreement; a maximum of \(1/k\) does not prove its absence.” The same omission is in the title.
- §5.1: “worse at nDCG@10 by 0.001 to 0.010 on five collections.” Table 6 prints ArguAna bm25-or-raw 0.473 and bm25-or 0.484; under your rounding rule that is 0.011. Either the range ends at 0.011 or the rule needs an exception. (The same paragraph's “905 against 52” is 17.4×, not 18×, unless another collection supplies the 18.)
- §5.1: “on which no quoted token matches any document.” The fixed builder does not quote. “No token” is what the raw-OR check shows.
- The abstract uses “the ceiling” three times before §2 defines it. Define it in one clause there, or write “a maximum of \(1/k\)” until §2.
- “Content tokens” and “exact-match list” are used without definition, the latter in §5.3 and §5.5. Define both once, or drop the labels.
- The Introduction and “What to log” claim no more than the body; the contribution list's “22,362 hybrid query runs” is the count for both hybrid conditions, while the empty-rate statistic is measured on 11,181 buggy-builder runs. Say “of the 22,362 runs, the 11,181 buggy-builder runs” once.

## Q3. Structure

Leading with the property works for cs.IR; the incident does not read as an afterthought because Table 1 is the only production evidence and arrives early. Section 4 is the right length; §4.3 is the paper's best writing. Keep “What to log” as its own section before Limitations — it is the actionable payload, and folding it into the conclusion would bury it. Cut entirely: Appendix A (two negative results, irrelevant to the thesis), §5.4 (replace with two sentences and Figure 1; Table 4 to the repository), two of the three per-encoder Appendix B tables, and the fourteen-model list in “Use of AI tools” (keep the disclosure, drop the names). That is roughly the four pages you want.

## Q4. Title

The current title is true of the derivation and false of the experiments: “Agree on Nothing” is the \(K \le k\) equivalence, and the paper's own runs at \(K=100\) produce two counterexamples. It also hides that the contribution is a diagnostic, not a theorem. Plain: **“Detecting a Silent Keyword Retriever in Hybrid Search.”** Names the finding: **“The \(1/k\) Ceiling: A Zero-Overlap Test for Hybrid Retrieval.”** I would use the second, with the \(K \le k\) condition stated in the abstract.

## Q5. Prior art

(a) The bound is a two-line consequence of Cormack, Clarke and Büttcher (2009); I know of no paper that states it as a zero-overlap test. Bruch, Gai and Ingber (2022; TOIS 2024) analyse fusion functions and are the closest work; you cite them. Fox and Shaw (1994), Lee (1997), Montague and Aslam (2001) and Webber, Moffat and Zobel (2010) are the right lineage and are cited. (b) QPP is the academic analogue: Cronen-Townsend, Zhou and Croft (2002) is cited; Carmel and Yom-Tov, *Estimating the Query Difficulty for Information Retrieval* (2010), is the standard reference you omit. Cooper (1998) is the right monitoring citation and is cited. I cannot name a production paper that logs per-retriever counts and maximum fused score; vendor documentation on RRF and hybrid search describes fusion parameters, not monitoring, and I am not confident enough to cite a specific page. (c) I know of no peer-reviewed report of a silently inert retriever in a hybrid system. FTS5's implicit AND is documented and cited; natural-language queries returning zero under an AND operator are a familiar practitioner failure in FTS5, Elasticsearch and Weaviate, but I cannot name a specific issue report with confidence — treat that as unsure. On this evidence the incident is the novel part.

## Q6. Would I post it?

Yes, after the conclusion fix and the \(K \le k\) sentence in the abstract. An IR reader will finish §§1–2, §4 and §§5.1–5.3; §5.4 is where they stop, because it re-proves a point Figure 1 has already made on two small collections. The one change that most improves finishing is to cut §5.4 to a paragraph and move Table 4 to the repository. The conclusion contradiction is the one change that most protects the paper's credibility, and it is not optional.
