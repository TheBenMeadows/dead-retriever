1. **Section 2.** The equivalence is correct for complete RRF scores, positive \(k\), and \(K\leq k\): a singleton scores at most \(1/k\), while any shared document scores at least \(2/(k+K-1)>1/k\). With at least one result and no overlap, the maximum is **exactly** \(1/k\), not merely at most \(1/k\).

   Two sentences need correction. “With more than two retrievers the test detects at most one live leg” is false: three live, pairwise-disjoint lists also meet the ceiling. The test detects *pairwise zero overlap* under \(K\leq k\), regardless of \(L\). And “the ceiling says the fusion is not fusing” is imprecise: RRF still merges disjoint rankings; it does not reinforce any shared document. For \(K>k\), calling the descending \(1/k,1/(k+1),\ldots\) sequence a “fingerprint of a single list” needs a qualification about observing the complete score sequence, including multiplicities; a run of those values alone does not establish one live leg.

   The useful missing one-sided result is: **for any \(K\), \(\max s(d)>1/k\) proves overlap**. The converse fails when \(K>k\); for two ranks \(r_1,r_2\), their combined score exceeds \(1/k\) exactly when \(r_1r_2<k^2\). Consequently, the closing claim that the “only thing to calibrate” is disjoint live lists is too broad for Section 6’s \(K=100>60\): deep overlap can also remain at or below the ceiling. The one-based qualification could add that its strict-separation condition is \(K\leq k+1\).

2. **Section 6.** The empty-leg accounting is arithmetically sound: \(2(300+323)\times2=2{,}492\) hybrid query runs; NFCorpus has \(167-164=3\) excess buggy-builder alarms among 159 live-leg queries, and \(28-25=3\) and \(27-25=2\) among 298 after repair. Given the stated per-query zero-overlap checks, those particular excess alarms correctly identify *no shared documents*. They remain false positives **for an empty-leg alarm**. “The alarm was literally right” obscures that distinction, especially at \(K>k\), where the score alone cannot establish zero overlap. The claimed false-positive rate depends on list content and ranks as well as live-list length.

   The NFCorpus sentence saying that “there” (on the 159 live queries) hybrid-AND differs from dense by 0.008 nDCG and 0.005 recall assigns **whole-collection** differences to the subset. For bge, the whole-collection differences are 0.00754 and 0.00466; because empty-leg queries have identical rankings, the corresponding means across the 159 live queries are approximately **0.0153 and 0.00946**.

   The effectiveness paragraph switches baselines mid-claim. If “repairing” means hybrid-OR versus hybrid-AND, bge NFCorpus Recall@100 **falls** from 0.3414 to 0.3373, rather than changing by the cited +0.001 (which compares hybrid-OR with dense). Nomic NFCorpus recall rises from 0.3030 to 0.3088, not from 0.298 to 0.309; the latter also uses dense as baseline. State both comparisons separately. The three-of-four nDCG result is correct, but “where the AND builder fired ... BM25 is at its best” and “it stayed silent where it would have added noise” are causal explanations not established by these aggregate tables. They require paired, query-stratified effectiveness results.

   “No more effective” for raw OR should specify **nDCG@10**: raw OR has slightly higher SciFact Recall@100 (0.879 versus 0.876).

3. **Prose.** Cut “The ceiling says the fusion is not fusing”; it both repeats and misstates the overlap result. “What the test costs ... What it buys ...” is promotional where a precise detection condition would do. Section 6 states the leg-health/effectiveness distinction before Table 2 and again in its last sentence; keep one. Replace “the alarm was literally right” with the measured zero-overlap fact. “This system had neither” enlarges the claim beyond these measurements.

4. **PC judgment.** The contribution is a useful failure case and an elementary RRF diagnostic, **not a new fusion method or a substantial theoretical result**. Search-system engineers and IR practitioners would read it. It is worth posting as an arXiv preprint once the comparator and wording errors are fixed. I would not accept this evidence as a SIGIR or ECIR full research paper; an industry, demo, or short-paper venue is plausible if its case-study criteria fit. The single change with the largest payoff is a larger, query-level diagnostic evaluation across multiple real hybrid systems: compare the score alarm against per-leg counts and direct overlap, and report when it gives an operator information those already-available measurements do not.
