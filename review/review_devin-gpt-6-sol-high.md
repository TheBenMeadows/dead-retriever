## Ranked findings

1. **The NFCorpus ceiling excess is a valid false-positive count, but its stated cause is not established.** In `main.tex` §6, the differences are \(0.517028-0.507740=3/323\) and \(0.083591-0.077399=2/323\). An empty keyword list necessarily produces a maximum of \(1/k\) here because dense returns a rank-0 result, so the empty-query set is contained in the ceiling-query set. Subtracting the rates is valid; the sets cannot differ in both directions. But at \(K=100>k=60\), a ceiling query can **have overlapping documents**: one document at rank 99 in both lists scores \(2/159<1/60\), while a nonoverlapping rank-0 document sets the maximum to \(1/60\). Delete “happened to share nothing” unless per-query `multi_leg_docs` establishes it for those five queries. The result files contain only means, not that cross-tabulation.

2. **Separate the exact theorem from the proposed diagnosis.** The §2 implication is correct for positive \(k\), unique document identities, and scores over the fused union: with integer \(K\leq k\), \(2/(k+K-1)>1/k\). There is no counterexample under those assumptions. The converse for a nonempty union also holds: no overlaps implies maximum \(1/k\). It does **not** follow that one retriever is empty. Both can return disjoint full lists; indeed the incident reports four queries with keyword hits and no overlap. The abstract’s “signature of a retriever that returns nothing,” §2’s “almost always share,” and the practical instruction “If it is \(1/k\) more than occasionally, one of your retrievers is returning nothing” turn a useful alert into an unsupported diagnosis. Recommend checking per-leg counts and overlap, not inferring emptiness from scores alone.

   The \(K>k\) weak form needs a tie qualification. A maximum exactly \(1/k\) guarantees **some** rank-0 singleton when a list is nonempty, not that every tied top document is a singleton: ranks 60 and 60 give \(1/120+1/120=1/60\). Nor is the staircase \(1/k,1/(k+1),\ldots\) uniquely a single-list fingerprint without assumptions about ties and the observed depth.

3. **Correct the interpretation of the BEIR outcomes.** In §6, equal SciFact Recall@100 for `dense` and `hybrid-and` does not mean their retrieved lists are identical; 6 of 300 keyword queries are nonempty, and nDCG differs (0.737626 versus 0.738556 for BGE). Say the lists are identical *on the 294 empty-keyword queries*. “Otherwise within noise” has no basis without paired uncertainty estimates or repeated runs. The tables also support a conclusion worth stating directly: repairing the leg increases overlap substantially but does not improve BGE nDCG@10 on either collection; on NFCorpus it **does** slightly improve BGE Recall@100 (0.332002 to 0.336478). For Nomic, SciFact Recall@100 rises by 0.030000 and NFCorpus by 0.011119, despite little or negative nDCG change. Leg health and retrieval effectiveness are distinct results. Put that distinction in the abstract or opening of §6, not only after the table.

4. **Code implements the main comparison, with qualifications that affect interpretation.** In `hybrid.py`, `fts_query(..., "and_quoted")` does reproduce FTS5’s implicit AND between quoted phrases. `KeywordIndex.search` catches **every** `sqlite3.OperationalError`, not just malformed MATCH syntax: an operational failure becomes an apparently empty keyword leg. `ceiling_report` counts overlap across the full fused union, but `at_ceiling` tests `mx <= 1/k + 1e-12`, not exact equality; an entirely empty run has maximum zero and is marked at ceiling if two legs are configured. Neither changes these reported BEIR rates given nonempty dense runs, but narrow its name or specify its preconditions. `rrf_fuse` credits the first occurrence of a duplicate within a leg and skips later occurrences; it does **not** enforce depth itself, and duplicate positions leave rank gaps. The runner’s searches do enforce depth 100, so this is not evidence of bias in the four files.

   In `beir_run.py`, title-plus-text dense input, separate FTS5 title/text fields, Nomic query/document prefixes, shared depth, and filtering to queries with qrels are applied consistently across conditions. BGE receives no query instruction; this affects the BGE baseline choice, not one hybrid condition selectively. More consequential for reproducibility, cached vectors and FTS indexes are reused solely by filename without validating corpus contents, query ordering, prefixes, or index population. State that results require a fresh cache or validate it. Do not claim stale caches caused these results.

5. **The public numbers check out; the private ones cannot be checked from the supplied files.** Every displayed value in Tables 2 and 3 matches its corresponding JSON to the shown rounding, including counts of 294/300 SciFact empty queries, 164/323 NFCorpus buggy-empty queries, and 25/323 fixed-empty queries. The prose’s “within one percentage point,” “three queries,” “two queries,” and Nomic SciFact three-point recall gain also check out. Table 1 and the incident, timing, recency, and prefix figures have **no supporting result file among the four provided**; distinguish “not contradicted” from “auditable.” The absolute adjacent-score gap is
   \[
   \frac1{k+r}-\frac1{k+r+1}
   =\frac1{(k+r)(k+r+1)}\approx\frac1{k^2}
   \]
   for \(r\ll k\). That is not itself **1.4%**: the relative drop is \(1/(k+r+1)\), about 1.64% at \(r=0,k=60\), and about 1.4% near \(r=10\). Make the units explicit in §1.

6. **Tighten citation scope and remove claims the citations cannot carry.** The listed RRF, BEIR, collection, FTS5, and fusion references have no evident title/author/venue error from the supplied bibliography; without external checking, I would not certify their metadata. `xiao2023cpack` describes *Chinese* embedding resources, so it is an indirect citation for the specific English `bge-base-en-v1.5` checkpoint; `nussbaum2024nomic` likewise needs a version-specific source if it is meant to substantiate v1.5 settings. Cormack et al. support the choice of \(k=60\), **not** the claim that it survived unchanged into “most hybrid-search implementations.” BEIR does not establish that silently dropping BM25 necessarily costs effectiveness: your BGE nDCG rows demonstrate otherwise. Add a source if claiming prior work on operational zero-hit/overlap monitoring, or describe that practice without a novelty implication; avoid inventing a citation.

   Cut §4’s “second lesson” about one investigation and patch per copy, which the measurements do not establish. Cut “the test costs nothing” in §2: logging and interpretation have costs, even if the score comparison is cheap. The limitations about \(K>k\), private uncheckable aggregates, and lack of paired significance belong beside the first relevant claim. Replace §7’s “an FTS layer that returns zero rows for a six-word query is broken, not selective” with a check recommendation: zero hits can be legitimate.
