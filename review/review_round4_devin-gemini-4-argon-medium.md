### 1. Defects, inconsistencies and claims wider than the evidence

- **False explanation of why `hybrid-and` beats `hybrid-or` in 12 of 18 pairs (Section 6.5).** The text follows "the buggy hybrid is above the repaired one at nDCG@10 in 12" with: *"A plausible mechanism, not tested here, is that where the AND builder fired at all it returned a short list of exact matches that ranked well at depth 10, while the OR list adds candidates that help at depth 100 when the dense leg misses them and dilute depth 10 when it does not."* In 9 of those 12 pairs (SciFact bge; ArguAna bge; SciDocs all three; FiQA all three; TREC-COVID nomic), `hybrid-and` does not beat `dense` (or beats it by 0.001 on six queries in SciFact bge): on ArguAna `hybrid-and` equals `dense` to six decimals because the single shared document is the non-relevant self-match, on SciDocs it is empty on 94.2% of queries and 0.000--0.002 below `dense`, and on FiQA and TREC-COVID `hybrid-and` is 0.010 and 0.039 *below* `dense`. In those 9 pairs `hybrid-and` beats `hybrid-or` because an mostly empty leg leaves a stronger dense ranking undisturbed while unweighted RRF with a weaker OR leg dilutes top-10 precision. Only on NFCorpus (all three encoders) does `hybrid-and` meaningfully exceed `dense` (+0.008 to +0.013).
- **Apparent table-vs-prose mismatches from rounding (Section 6.1, 6.3, 6.5 vs Tables 3, 4, 6--8).** Several prose figures are computed from unrounded JSON floats and disagree by 1 unit in the last place (or 1 count) with the three-decimal tables directly above them:
  - **Section 6.5 vs Table 4:** *"above it at Recall@100 in 9"*. In Table 4, only **8** pairs show `h-or` > `dense`; the ninth (NFCorpus bge: `0.337309` vs `0.336722`) prints as `0.337` vs `0.337`.
  - **Section 6.3 vs Table 3:** *"Over the whole collection [SciFact] the two conditions differ by at most 0.002 nDCG@10"*. In Table 3, SciFact e5 is `0.719` (`dense`) vs `0.722` (`h-and`), a table difference of `0.003` (`0.721622 - 0.719429 = 0.002193`).
  - **Section 6.3 vs Table 4:** *"and on SciDocs, with 942 empty legs, they differ by at most 0.002 and 0.000"*. In Table 4, SciDocs bge Recall@100 is `0.496` (`dense`) vs `0.495` (`h-and`), a table difference of `0.001` (`0.495683 - 0.495483 = 0.000200`).
  - **Section 6.5 vs Table 3:** ArguAna e5 gain is `"e5 +0.059"` (`0.389 - 0.329 = +0.060` in Table 3); SciFact nomic is `"+0.004"` (`0.708 - 0.703 = +0.005` in Table 3); FiQA bge loss is `"0.059 below dense"` (`0.406 - 0.348 = 0.058` in Table 3); Section 6.3 NFCorpus `hybrid-and` gain is `"0.008 to 0.013"` (`0.361 - 0.347 = 0.014` on nomic in Table 3); Section 6.1 FiQA Recall@100 difference is `"0.012 Recall@100"` (`0.521 - 0.508 = 0.013` in Tables 6--8).
- **0-based vs 1-based rank indexing between Sections 2 and 6.2.** Section 2 defines $r_\ell(d) \in \{0,\ldots,K-1\}$ and writes `"rank 0"` and `"every retriever's rank-0 document is exclusive"`. Section 6.2 writes `"(at rank one on 1,296)"` for the top position.
- **Production vs BEIR document granularity in Section 6.1.** *"Every quoted token must appear in one 1,400-character chunk or one abstract"* conflates the production chunking (Section 1) with BEIR documents, which are neither chunked at 1,400 characters nor all abstracts (FiQA is forum posts, ArguAna is debate arguments).
- **Omitted collection in the short-list breakdown (Section 6.4).** *"Pooled over every collection except ArguAna... 137 of the 726 live-leg query runs in which the leg returned one to three documents (18.9%)... 13 of 330 on NFCorpus (3.6k documents), 18 of 141 on SciDocs (26k), 90 of 219 on FiQA (58k) and 16 of 18 on TREC-COVID (171k)."* The denominators sum to 708, not 726, because SciFact (`0 of 18`, 5.2k documents) is silently omitted from the per-collection list even though it sits between NFCorpus and SciDocs in corpus size.

### 3. Interpretation checks

- **(a) Empty rate ordered by content tokens across five collections:** Properly hedged (*"Five collections are too few to call this a law, and corpus size, document length and vocabulary differ along the same ordering"*).
- **(b) Short-list disjointness growing with corpus size:** Properly hedged in Sections 6.4 and 8, provided SciFact's `0 of 18` at 5.2k documents is included in Section 6.4 rather than omitted.
- **(c) ArguAna self-match generalising to real systems:** Sound and well-bounded. Indexing queries is routine in email, notes and ticket search; the text claims only that such systems can exhibit the same blind spot if a query is pasted from an indexed item.
- **(d) "A monitor for leg health and a verdict on retrieval effectiveness are different instruments":** Exactly supported by the data (overlap rises from $\le 4.0$ to $19.0\text{--}43.7$ everywhere, while nDCG@10 beats `dense` in only 5 of 18 pairs), once the flawed mechanism sentence in Section 6.5 is fixed.

### 4. Journal fit (ranked by impact on acceptance)

1. **Paired significance tests on Tables 3 and 4 (Required).** An IRRJ referee will not accept a disclaimer caption (*"no paired uncertainty is reported"*) when the per-query scores are already on disk in `results/*.perquery.jsonl`; add a paired permutation or Wilcoxon/t-test on `h-or` vs `dense` and `h-and` vs `dense` so the reader knows which of the 5 gains and 13 losses are noise (especially the $\pm 0.002$ differences on SciFact and SciDocs and the 50-query TREC-COVID runs).
2. **Correct the mechanism claim in Section 6.5 (Required).** Separate the 3 NFCorpus runs where `hybrid-and` genuinely improves over `dense` from the 9 runs where `hybrid-and` beats `hybrid-or` only by abstaining on 74--100% of queries while unweighted RRF with a weak BM25 leg degrades `dense`.
3. **Conventional paper roadmap and section titles (Optional).** Sections 1--5 read as a post-mortem narrative before Section 6 turns into a BEIR study; adding a two-sentence roadmap at the end of Section 1 explaining why Section 5's negative results are included will keep referees from flagging Section 5 as a digression.
4. **One visual figure (Optional).** A scatter or bar plot of per-collection `kw empty` vs `at ceiling` (and the 1-3 vs 4-49 row-count buckets) would make the excess-ceiling regime and the ArguAna blind spot immediately visible.

### 5. Abstract and title

The title and abstract match the body's scope and numbers without overclaiming. One phrasing tightening in the abstract: *"with one shared document deep in both lists in two, the case depth $K > k$ leaves open"* is slightly elliptical on first reading; changing `"in two"` to `"in two runs"` makes the contrast with `"in 174 runs"` immediate.
