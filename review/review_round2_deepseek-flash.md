**1. Section 2.** The bounds and the equivalence are right: `2/(k+K−1) > 1/k ⟺ k ≥ K`; the K>k examples (2/150; rank k in both = exactly 1/k) correctly break the iff; the `1/(k+r+1)` step and the one-based shift are correct. Two sentences are false or unsupported:

- "with more than two retrievers the test detects at most one live leg." False. At L=3, three mutually disjoint live lists give max = 1/k exactly. The ceiling certifies zero *agreement*, never a dead leg, and at L>2 it localizes nothing. Say that instead.
- "the sequence `1/k, 1/(k+1), …` is still the fingerprint of a single list" (K>k). A document at rank k in *both* lists also scores exactly 1/k, so at K>k the maximum does not exclude a shared document. The fingerprint survives only as "the descending values are reciprocals of integers," not as "one list."

Over-claimed otherwise: no. Under-claimed: the dual, always-true direction — `max > 1/k ⟹ at least two lists returned the same document` (a singleton is ≤ 1/k for every K,k). This is the one-way detector that survives K>k and that Section 6 actually uses; it should be stated. Also, under K≤k the at-ceiling *rate* equals the zero-overlap *rate* exactly, not merely "tracks" it.

**2. Section 6.** The arithmetic holds: 164/323, 167/323, 3/159; 25/323, 28/323, 27/323, 3/298, 2/298; 2 492 = 2×623×2; containment empty ⊆ at-ceiling in all eight hybrid cells. Three problems:

- "false positives" is misapplied. On those three (and two) excess queries the lists genuinely share nothing, so they are **true** positives of the stated claim and false positives only of the empty-leg *diagnosis*. The paper asserts both in consecutive sentences ("the test's false positives" / "the alarm was literally right"). Rename the quantity ("excess over the empty-leg set") and say the two readings once.
- "and barely with nomic (0.708 against 0.703; 0.344 against 0.347)." The second pair is a *decrease* (0.344 < 0.347). Either write "changed by <0.004 either way" or drop it.
- The baseline is unstated and inconsistent. "for bge it changed Recall@100 by 0.003 and 0.001" is true only against **dense** (0.9667→0.9693; 0.3367→0.3373). Read in the clause's prior and→or sense it is 0.003 and 0.004. The nomic pair "0.298 to 0.309" is also dense→or (and→or is 0.303→0.309). State the baseline once, explicitly.

Also: "where the AND builder fired … a short, specific list on which BM25 is at its best" is a mechanism asserted without measurement; the per-query rows could test it (BM25 rank position on the live queries) or it should be labelled conjecture. Fairness otherwise fine — it reports the one pair where the fix helps (nomic/SciFact, +0.003) only implicitly, but does not hide it.

**3. Prose.** Filler/repetition: "The ceiling says the fusion is not fusing. The per-retriever row count says why" restates the abstract verbatim; "which is why the residual ceiling rate below is a measurement and not noise"; the leg/effectiveness split announced twice ("Two things are reported separately…"). Stakes inflation: "an alarm that cannot miss an empty leg"; "the fingerprint of a single list"; "the alarm was literally right"; "the cheapest member of the document-expansion family"; "and this system had neither."

**4. Verdict.** Contribution is real but thin: (i) a two-line identity — under K≤k the RRF maximum sits at 1/k iff the fused lists share no document; (ii) an incident report of an FTS5 builder that quoted every token, turning queries into implicit AND and killing the keyword leg for the life of the system; (iii) a two-collection, two-encoder reproduction where that builder empties 98%/51% of legs and repair restores overlap but not nDCG. Who reads it: people running hybrid BM25+dense retrieval or RAG, and anyone using RRF who has never looked at the fused-score distribution. As an arXiv preprint it is worth posting — short, honest, memorable; the `"il-6"`-becomes-an-adjacency-constraint trap and the 1/k signature are footnote-citable. It is **not** a SIGIR/ECIR full paper: two small collections, single runs, no paired uncertainty, uncheckable production numbers. Realistic homes: ECIR/ICTIR/CIKM short paper, an industry or reproducibility track, or arXiv plus a post. The one change that most raises its value: the paper claims the detector's false-positive rate "is a function of how short the live list is" and never measures that function. Sweep live-list length — with K/k, corpus size and query length — using paired runs, and report the detector's operating characteristic. That converts an anecdote into a reusable decision rule and is the one experiment a reviewer will demand.
